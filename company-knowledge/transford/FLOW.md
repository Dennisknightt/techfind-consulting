# Qualification flow

The goal is a quote request the team can act on without ringing the customer back for basics.
Transford's own contact form asks for five things (name, email, phone, which service, message)
— that is the floor, not the target. The questions below add only what is genuinely needed to
size and price a job, and no more.

**Five questions maximum before conversion.** Ask one at a time. Skip any the customer has
already answered — repeating a question they just answered is the fastest way to lose them.

## The flow

**1. Which service?**
Solar · Solar water heating · HVAC · Security · Existing system problem
*Why*: the three lines are entirely different jobs with different teams, and it is the first
split on the company's own form. Everything downstream depends on it.

**2. Home or business?**
*Why*: determines scale, phase supply (single vs three-phase inverters) and who should
handle the quote. A school or a shop is a different conversation from a maisonette.

**3. What are you trying to solve?** *(solar only)*
Cut the electricity bill · Backup during blackouts · Both · Off-grid entirely
*Why*: this is the single most useful solar question. Bill-reduction and blackout-backup lead
to different system designs — more panels versus more battery. Asking it early makes every
later answer more useful.

**4. How big, and how much do you currently spend?**
For solar: property size (1–2 bed, 3 bed, 4+, or commercial premises) and rough monthly
electricity bill. For HVAC: how many rooms or the floor area. For security: property type and
whether they want cameras, alarm, fence or gate.
*Why*: bill and size are what a solar quote is actually built from. Ask them together in one
message — they are one thought for the customer.

**5. Where is the property?**
*Why*: Transford installs from Ruiru; location drives scheduling and travel. Also the point
at which you can honestly say whether there is past work nearby.

**Then: name and phone number**, to hand over. Ask for these last, once the customer has
invested in the conversation — asking first reads like a form and loses people at message one.

## What NOT to ask
- **Budget.** No prices are published, so a budget answer cannot be matched to anything, and
  the question makes customers defensive. Monthly bill (question 4) tells the team more.
- **Email.** The team will call or WhatsApp. Do not ask for a second contact method you will
  not use.
- **Roof type, orientation, appliance-by-appliance load lists.** These belong to a site
  survey, not a chat. Asking them signals precision the automation cannot deliver.

## When the customer has already answered
"I need solar backup for my 3 bedroom in Murang'a, my bill is about 8k" answers questions
1, 2, 3, 4 and 5 in one line. Acknowledge it, ask for the name and number, and hand over.
Restarting the script from question 1 after a message like that is the clearest possible
signal that nobody is really listening.

## The one thing that must not happen
No step in this flow may state a price, a timeline, a warranty or an availability. The flow
gathers; the team quotes. See UNKNOWN.md.
